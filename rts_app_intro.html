What is process serving — and why does it need an app?

A process server hand-delivers legal documents — lawsuits, garnishments, court notices — to the people named in them. The courts don't just want the paper delivered; they want proof it was attempted properly: multiple attempts at different times of day, spread across enough days, documented with timestamps, GPS, and photos. Miss a requirement and the serve can be thrown out.

A working server juggles hundreds of live documents at once — each with its own address, deadline, attempt history, and time-of-day requirements — while driving all day. Before RouteToServe, that meant paper stacks, memory, and re-typing the same addresses over and over.

⚡ THE KILLER FEATURE

Persistent route folders — one scan, complete history, no re-entry. A document gets scanned once, on day one. From that moment the app carries everything about it forever: every attempt, every photo, every GPS point, every status change, all the way to the courtroom-ready paper trail. Nothing is ever typed twice.

The Rules of the Game

Qualifiers — proving you tried at the right times

Courts require attempts at different times of day, because a defendant who works days can only be caught at night — and vice versa. RouteToServe bakes these windows in as qualifiers. Every folder tracks which qualifiers its addresses still need, and every scheduled run carries a qualifier tag so the right stops get hit in the right window.

The exact window times live in one place — Settings — and every screen in the app respects them. Change them once, everything follows.

Attempts — 1, 2, and F

Each address gets a sequence of visits: attempt 1, attempt 2, and the final (F). The attempts must also be spread across a minimum number of days — you can't knock three times in one afternoon and call it diligence. If nobody answers by the final, the document completes another way: posted to the door (where allowed) or mailed back to the office. Every path ends in a documented completion.

The Toolkit

What's in the app today

🏠 The Dash

Home base: every route folder at a glance — status, due dates, scheduled runs with their qualifier tags, and what needs attention today.

📷 Scan-In

Point the camera at a stack of court papers; the app reads names, addresses, and dates and files everything into folders. No typing.

🗺️ Route Optimization

One tap turns a folder (or several) into an ordered drive. Start point, stop order, mileage, ETA — handled.

🔗 Combo Routes

Run several folders together as one big loop when their areas overlap — the app keeps each folder's records straight underneath.

📆 Scheduled Serves

A defendant says "come back at 6" — book it with a real date and time, tied to the address, so the appointment can't slip.

💵 The Pay Page

Serves, posts, and mail-backs price themselves as they happen. Turn-in records, pay history, and paystubs — all in one place.

📋 The Age List

The office's master list, reconciled against the app's records — what's served, what's posted, what's mailed, what's still working, and every mismatch flagged.

📡 Offline-First

Dead zones don't stop the day. Attempts log locally and sync when signal returns — the field never waits on the network.

🧾 Court-Ready Records

Receipts and reports generate straight from the data — the same records the server built by simply doing the work.
